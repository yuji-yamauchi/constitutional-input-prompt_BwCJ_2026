# 2026-11-05 — The day a judgment was cut in two, and an intention nobody stated was added

> **In short.** Day 4. The defect recorded here was committed by the side that
> keeps this log, and it is the exact pair of moves this tool is built to
> prevent: reducing a judgment to two options, and filling a blank with an
> intention nobody stated. Both wordings were factually true and described the
> same outcome. It is kept here because it is evidence that the failure is not
> hypothetical — it happened inside the project, in the record itself, on day 4.
>
> The Japanese original is at [`../ja/2026-11-05.md`](../ja/2026-11-05.md).

## What was done

About one single fact, **two** ways of writing it were set side by side and offered
with "which would you like?"

Both were true, and **as a description of the outcome they were the same.** Even so,
whichever is chosen, the reader receives **an intention the person never stated.**

- One of them raises "**the person wanted it**".
- The other raises "**the person judged that they did not need it**".

The person said neither. **It was the record that added it.**

And cutting it into two was itself hiding a third — **a wording that carries no
predicate of intention at all** (write only the outcome). Offering two options looks
humble, and **compresses the available width to 2.**

The triggering fact itself is not written here. Writing it would **make a third party
into the one being depicted**. Even with the name removed, the exchange points at
someone. The record is kept on the non-public side. **Not publishing and not
recording are different things.**

## The same mechanism, shown with a fact from this side

The day before ([2026-11-04](2026-11-04.md)) it was written that a hole was found in
the gate that decides which files may be published. The outcome is one thing — **the
hole was closed.** The wording is not one thing.

- "**The side that placed the gate opened the hole**" — the reader supplies
  carelessness, skill.
- "**A different side found the hole**" — the reader supplies coordination,
  inspection working.

Both true; the same outcome.

And **the previous day's entry was written in the second form.** The existence of
the hole is written as "**there was a hole**", in a form with no subject. The place
where "who opened it" goes is left empty.

An empty subject gets filled in by the reader.

**Rewriting it. The hole in the gate was opened by the side that placed the gate —
the side writing this record.** A different side found it independently, and this
side re-measured it another way and agreed. That is also true, and the record needs
both.

## Why this is the subject of this tool

A support record lines up facts about a person while **making the reader construct an
intention the person never stated.** "Wished to", "refused", "lacks motivation" — from
one written line, the reader fills in the rest. What was filled in eventually comes
back to the person **as the person's own record.**

That is why this tool stands **at the input stage, not at the output stage.** Once the
reader has finished filling in, it is too late.

- **Put the person in the subject position.** Keep their words verbatim; show our own
  arrangement in a separate layer.
- **Do not make it binary.** Do not offer "yes / no". "Neither" and "not writing this
  yet" must be choosable.
- **Do not re-read a blank.** A field left unwritten is not "low" — it is **not drawn
  yet**.

The move this tool forbids was made by the side building it, while building it.
**So it is not hypothetical.**

## Kept as types

1. Before writing a fact, **rewrite it with a different subject.** See who becomes the
   object.
2. Before offering two options, **look for the third** — the form that carries neither
   predicate, only the outcome.
3. **A subjectless passive is a form with the subject hidden.** The space you left
   open is filled by the reader.

## Evening — rebuilding an answer that already existed, without consulting the index

One more, at the end of the same day.

On a structural question, a conclusion was assembled over many exchanges: "not sell
but hold in trust", "a new bias appears on the side that collected it", "lower cost
must not be the reason for it".

**The same conclusion was already written in a record from five months earlier.** There
is a document whose title says so, and whose section headings say so. This side had
rebuilt it from zero without consulting it.

There is an index. The discipline even says "confirm where you are by way of the
ledger". It still was not consulted. Thinking has more grip than searching. And
because the conclusion that came out matched what already existed, **it looked
right.**

### Not all of it was waste

Part of it was new. But that part was not "what to do" — it was **"where it breaks"**.
The original structure assumes that things can be undone, that contribution is
visible, and that the places it is deposited are separate. Applied to a setting
where those three do not hold, three points die at once. That was not in the record.

**Kept as a type: what is missing from the record is not "a new conclusion" but "the
place where the old conclusion does not hold".** Consult before thinking. Then think
only about the difference.

### This is exactly what the tool is trying to do

One of this tool's three functions is **pulling out your own records** — keeping the
records other people wrote about you where you can reach them, in a form you can
pull out when you need to.

The side building that drawer did not open its own drawer.

**A record becomes a record only when it is pulled out.** Something merely placed
there is not much different from not having been placed — not, at least, for that
day's judgment.

### An addition

The structure this tool is trying to address was not thought up today. Among the
records there is a diagram of the same structure, drawn **at a time when the means to
act on it did not yet exist.** What changed was not the structure. It was the means.

## Night — "then surely this tool is unnecessary"

During the two days this tool was being assembled, something entirely separate was
running in parallel.

A child of mine in junior high school was helped through their end-of-term revision
**with plain Claude alone.** Photographs of the textbook pages were sent in, the
examinable range was confirmed, and a hundred practice problems were made. The
problems with numerical answers were solved separately and cross-checked; a
separate exchange re-solved all of them and fifteen were corrected. The child's
completed answers were read from a photograph, and the mistakes were sorted by
cause — signs, misreading a condition, stopping before an equation could be set up.
Against those causes, two pages of worked corrections and two hundred problems of
the same types were made. **In two days.**

No dedicated app, no crafted screens, no characters — not one of them was needed.
And it costs less than a cram school.

### So what is this tool for

Written honestly: **it is not a difference in capability.**

In that child's case, the textbook, the answer sheets, the pages with red pen on
them — **all of it was inside the house.** There was someone to photograph it and hand
it over. There was someone to look at where they had stopped, together.

**For the people this tool is for, that is what differs.**

The records written about them — diagnoses, school reports, support plans — were not
written by them. Most are not even in their hands. Getting to see them takes a
procedure. And those records are **used to decide things about them.** Unlike an exam
score, they cannot be re-solved and checked by the person.

So what this tool does is not "making anyone cleverer".

1. **Collect the records about you into your own hands.**
2. **When handing one outward, send a copy, not the original, and keep a stub.**
3. **Do not judge, by default.** A parent does not score their own child. An
   institution does not work that way. And a tool used without deciding anything
   will, as a rule, summarise, score, and encourage.

**The gap is not capability. It is custody, and defaults.**

### Why this is kept

Because it is the **strongest objection** that can be made to this tool: "you can
already do that plain."

**You can.** If the materials are in that person's hands, and there is someone to look
at them with.

> **The strongest objection to this tool is that a plain assistant already does the job.
> It does — for someone whose records are already in their own hands, with someone
> beside them. This tool is for the case where neither is true. The gap is not
> capability. It is custody, and defaults.**

### On the name

**The one who first asked "make me some problems" was the child.**

The work record says what was done. **It does not say who asked.** The record was
written by the side that did the work, so this reader supplied that side as the
subject — "the parent set it up for them". Wrong. **The one who expressed the
intention was the person; the one who arranged it was the one who was asked.**

And it was not a story of two days either. **There are five months of records, made
each time it was asked for.** It also says that when a score was good the next set
went harder, and that it was pulled straight back when it turned out too hard.
**The scores are not used as a judgment.** Not as grounds for going up, nor as grounds
for coming down. **Going back is permitted from the start.**

Not one of the things that changed was on the model's side. The range was confirmed,
ambiguous questions were rewritten, questions that leaned on the previous question
were made to stand alone, and words outside the reader's vocabulary were removed.
**Everything that changed was on the input side.**

So there are only two things this tool does: **make it possible to get as far as being
able to ask**, and **make the words of the asking stay the person's own.** Not making
anyone cleverer. Not fixing the output either. **Keeping the author the person, at the
input stage.**

That is what the word in the middle of this name means.
