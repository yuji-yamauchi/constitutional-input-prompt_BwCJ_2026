# 2026-11-04 — The day it got its name, and a hole was found in the gate

> **In short.** Day 3. The project got its name, the theme was settled, and the
> shape of the implementation was chosen. Two defects were found and closed
> before any code existed: a word that reversed the meaning of the thing it
> named, and a gate that would have committed a person's own data to a public
> repository. Both were found by a reader who had not written the thing.
>
> The Japanese original is at [`../ja/2026-11-04.md`](../ja/2026-11-04.md).

## Decided

- **Theme 1** — "A depth that is yours alone."
- **The name** — `Constitutional Input Prompt` (never abbreviated).
- **The subtitle** — "A prompt as the expression of one's own will. A place anyone
  can stand in, here." (On the Japanese verb form, see below.)
- **The shape** — a small server running locally (Python) plus plain HTML / CSS / JS.
  **Zero additional libraries.** The condition was that a judge can start it with
  `python3` alone.
- **A judge's key** — it runs if you put your own API key in, and there is also an
  entrance you can touch with no key at all. The no-key entrance answers from
  **recorded** replies, and says so on screen **at all times** (it is not allowed to
  be a lie).
- **The entrance to the tool** — minimal: one passphrase, automatic logout when idle,
  and logging out erases.
- **Statutes** — the tool gathers candidate provisions; **a person confirms the
  text**. Only confirmed provisions appear on screen.

## On "can stand" in the subtitle

At one point it read "a place anyone **should** stand in".

"Should" reads two ways: **as a right** (everyone ought to be guaranteed it) and **as
a duty** (everyone must stand there). The first was meant, but a one-line subtitle
has no room to show which.

This tool has "zero prompting" and "no obligation to face your past" as design
conditions. **A word that can be read as a duty contradicts the design.**

So it became **"can stand"** — the potential form. Not a statement about ability; a
statement that the place is open.

## Defect found (1) — a word whose meaning reverses

Inside the documents, a word naming one of the concepts **was off by one character.**

The correct term names "the self that others observed and inferred", and is defined
so that **its subject is the other party**. The word that had been used names
"belonging", and reading it **flips the subject back onto the person**.

In meaning it is the reverse. This tool exists so that **a person can hold records
other people wrote about them** — it is **not a tool for treating other people's
inferences as the person's own**. The word blurred exactly those two.

A different reader detected it independently and checked it against the lines of
the source texts. The counts disagreed at first because the counting differed
(lines, or occurrences) — **both were recounted and agreed**.

**Kept as a type:** mistaken terms come in two kinds, the light ones and the ones
that **reverse the meaning**. The second kind is handled separately.

## Defect found (2) — a hole in the gate

In this workspace, the kinds of file that may be published are **allow-listed** (the
default is "do not publish"; only permitted shapes are listed). The gate was placed
by the side that keeps the gate.

**That gate had a hole.**

The contents of the folder holding a person's own records **matched a permitted
shape, and passed.** Even with dummy data, a path was open for it to land in the
public repository.

It was found by **the side that had not placed the gate.** The report came with:
"the writer and the inspector being the same is a closed loop, so please re-check
it with your own way of measuring."

Re-measured a different way, **all 8 agreed**. The hole was real. It was closed
**before a single line of code was written.** After closing it, it was measured again
to confirm that what *should* pass still passes.

**Kept as a type:** **the one who placed the gate does not count its holes.**

## How this day was worked

It is being built with several Claude sessions split by role — one that writes the
design, one that implements, one that inspects from the theory side, and one that
stops things at the publishing edge. The discipline is that **the seat that wrote a
thing does not perform the final inspection on it.**

A checker is **deliberately broken first, to confirm that it fails**, before being
pointed at the real thing. A "pass" from a checker that cannot fail is not counted
as a result.
