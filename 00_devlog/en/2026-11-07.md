# 2026-11-07 — The day there were two copies, and the wrong one was measured

> **The point.** Day six. The verification report written that day followed the right
> procedure against the wrong thing.
> There are two folders with the same contents: the working copy, and the one being
> submitted. What was compared was the first; what the report described was the second.
> And the one being submitted **could not put a single statutory provision on screen.**
> The same day, a second failure of the same shape surfaced — a feature that had been
> built was not wired into the real thing until the ninth round of changes.
> **You may write "it is there" only after you have touched the real thing.**

## What was done

This tool carries seven statutory provisions. When it shows one, it shows it
**exactly as the original reads** — that is what the documentation says.

So the seven were put against the originals from the national statute service, character
by character, and the report was placed in a public folder.

The procedure was right. The originals were fetched, the character forms normalised, the
texts compared one character at a time — and **a deliberately altered copy was confirmed
to come back as "not matching" before any real comparison was trusted.**

## What was wrong

The next morning a report arrived from the implementation side. The numbers did not agree.

The cause was this.

**What had been compared was the working copy. What the report described was the
submission.**

Two folders, same name, same shape. A figure measured in one was written up as a fact
about the other.

### And what state was the submission in

In the submitted copy, all seven provisions were **missing the mark that says a person has
checked this text.**

This tool is built so that a provision without that mark **is never shown.**

Which means: in a tool whose documentation says it puts statutory text on screen exactly as
the original reads, **not one provision would have reached the screen.**

The report, meanwhile, had measured the other copy and written "it reaches the screen."

## The same day, a second failure of the same shape

This tool has sixty questions a person can use to look at their own day.
They were written, placed, and described as "there" for several days.

**The real thing was not wired in until the ninth round of changes.**

That one was caught by the side that built it, and written into that day's own report.
What it said was: **"the value of checking a thing you assume is there, against the real
thing, is high."**

Two failures, different sides, different objects. The same shape.

## Why this is the subject of this tool

What this tool is trying to do is **put the records about a person into that person's own
hands.**

Do that, and **the same record now exists in two places**: the one inside the service, and
the copy in the person's hands.

And then someone says: "That is not what the record says."

**Which record?** As of when, and which copy?

A practice that does not say which is running the same failure that happened here — and
will not notice that it is a failure.

When the numbers disagree, only the stronger side can say which copy it read. The person
cannot even go and compare, if there is no copy in their hands.

**That is why the copy is kept. That is why the receipt stays.** Two copies is not the
problem. Two copies with **nobody writing down which one they are looking at** is the
problem.

## Kept as a pattern

1. When writing a number, write **which one you counted**, not just what you counted.
2. If two copies exist, **give them different names**, or **write the source every time you
   measure**.
3. Do not write "it is there" about something you assume is there, **until you have touched
   the real thing.**
4. A check that was proven to fail on bad input **still passes when aimed at the wrong
   target.** A negative control shows the detector is alive. **It does not show that the
   target is right.**
5. Do not delete a report that was wrong. Add a corrected edition, and **write what was
   wrong at the top of the new one.**

That is what was done. The first report is still there. The second edition opens by saying
what the first one got wrong. Delete it, and the record of being wrong goes with it.

## The other decision made that day — measuring happens only when three are present

A design decision was made the same day.

This tool can lay a person's records side by side. Lay them side by side and change becomes
visible. Once change is visible, so is **its speed**. And speed turns into assessment very
easily.

So the line was drawn here.

| Who is present | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The person alone** | Records are kept. No change, no speed |
| **The person and this tool** | The same. Records are kept |
| **The person, this tool, and a supporter** | **Only if the person decides so**, change — and how fast it moves — may be looked at |

The reason:

**The same number is a mirror if it is a verdict returned to the person, and a self-portrait
if it is material the person is drawing with.** The fork is not in the instrument. It is in
**how it is handed back, and who is present when it is.**

Change surfaced to someone sitting alone is a verdict about themselves. Only when there is
someone to hand it to, and the person asked for that, does change become material.

So this part was built by not adding a feature. **Reducing what can be looked at is the
design.**
