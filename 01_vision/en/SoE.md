## 0. The name is already a question

**Service of Empowerment.** And yet support that flies the flag of "empowerment"
**can invert into its opposite**: saying "for this person" while making them an
object; saying "giving power" while **never moving where the power sits**.

**SoE's blade always points this way** — **Empowerment, or Exploitation?**
That question has to be put repeatedly, **from inside**, not from outside.

The abbreviation carrying two meanings is not an accident. **It is a device for
keeping oneself under inspection.**

---

## 1. The axiom — the ethic of the floor

**SoE's axiom is not the maximisation of happiness. It is the removal of absolute
misfortune — the floor of dignity.**

- It takes a **sufficientarian** form. How high to raise things **above** the floor
  is not SoE's problem.
- **The definition of the floor carries the entire load.** Every rule in SoE
  descends from this axiom.

### Why only the floor

Because the moment "more happiness" becomes the goal, **support acquires a licence
to intervene in a person's life.** If only the floor is undertaken, **the scope of
intervention is structurally bounded.**

**The axiom sets the ceiling on paternalism.**

### The ordering discipline — do not stand it on its head

> **Dignity = the foundation, the condition. ROI, tax revenue, resilience = the
> result, the by-product.**

"Support produces taxpayers", "social resilience goes up" — these may be used as
**political arguments for institutionalisation. But they must never be made the
grounds for a human right.**

The moment they are, **a contrary figure authorises a rights violation.** When a
number says "resilience rises if you cut this group's rights", that line of
argument hands the weapon to the side doing the cutting.

### Prohibitions

- Do not set happiness **above** the floor as the goal of support (anti-perfectionism).
- **SoE does not undertake a duty to "cure" wants that have shrunk developmentally
  or environmentally.**

## 2. What human rights are placed upon

**SoE does not define "every human being has human rights" as a truth — not as a
natural right.**

Human rights = **a given arrived at by agreement** through democracy, the rule of
law, liberty, capital and scientific method: an institutional fact.

- Historically, periods in which human rights were inviolable have been **rare and
  local**. The present state of the world is also limited.
- In Japan today, **taking the Constitution and the ratification of the CRPD
  (Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities) as given**, they are
  operated as "rights that ought to be guaranteed".
- **Dignity is the content of the agreement; the ground is the agreement itself.**
  Dignity is not a metaphysical foundation.

### Why place it that way

**Because this is the meta version of goodwill-independence.** A right is not "a
truth of good intentions" but **a promise society has made.** So one can press the
institution for **performance of the promise**, rather than for individual goodwill.
Because no truth is assumed, **it does not close.**

### Holding the cost of that, knowingly

**It gives up the trump card of natural right.** If the agreement moves towards
un-agreement, there is no metaphysical veto. The defence is limited to three
layers.

1. **Immanent critique** — "You **promised**, in the Constitution and in the CRPD.
   Perform it."
2. **Public-health and resilience arguments** — supporting arguments, not grounds.
   Where they collide with the floor, the floor wins.
3. **The political process** — reforming the agreement. **Below that there is no
   safety net. It can be lost.**

**So in SoE, a human right is not a possession but an achievement that has to be
maintained.** The floor is not ground you stand on; it is **a waterline kept by
bailing, so as not to sink.**

## 3. The two selves

| | Definition | Whose |
|---|---|---|
| **Deductive self** | **The self as the person perceives it** (internal, first-person). **Thought and conduct are generated from this self.** | **The person** |
| **Inductive self** | **The self that others have observed and inferred** through a history of behaviour and records (external, third-person). | **Others** |

> The original proposition: **"The source of one's own conduct is deductive; the
> self as seen by others is inductive."**

### The deductive self can be distorted

This is where it is most often misunderstood.

The deductive self is not a principle the person **chose**. It is **"the self the
person perceives as the source of their conduct"** — a matter of perception, not
presupposing choice.

**So "I am a worthless person, therefore I will not try" is also a deductive
self.** It is, because it is in fact functioning as the source of conduct.

**Therefore the deductive self must not be idealised as "the real self" or "the
true self".**

### The inductive self is always on the other side

The picture a person assembles about themselves is not called the inductive self.
**The inductive self is the picture others build by observing.**

## 4. Adaptive preference — filling the void with someone else's definition

**Adaptive preference is not "folding one's wants smaller".** That is the surface.

**Adaptive preference is the mechanism by which a void in the self is filled with
someone else's definition.**
**It is a problem of self-concept before it is a problem of preference.**

### Why a void forms

Through education and the like, a deductive self is ordinarily cultivated, and the
boundary between one's own world and others' becomes clear.
**But a person whose educational opportunity was impaired is left with the
deductive self uncultivated.**

**A void forms there. A void has to be filled. And the material to fill it with is
close at hand — what others are saying about you.**

### Prohibitions

- **Do not say** "your wish is an adaptive preference" (i.e. "it is fake"). The
  concept is to be used only as a **shield**, never as a **sword**.
- Do not attribute adaptive preference to "weakness of the person's heart".
  **Do not psychologise a structure.**

---

## 5. Colonisation of self-concept

> A person whose educational opportunity was impaired, with the deductive self
> left uncultivated, **mistakes others' inductive self (the observed picture, the
> label) for their own deductive self, and internalises it.**

```
[educational opportunity impaired]
   ↓
[deductive self uncultivated]   ← not "an absence of self". A DEPRIVATION OF
   ↓                              INTERPRETIVE RESOURCES.
[a void forms]
   ↓   adaptive preference = "an adaptation that fills the missing deductive
   ↓    self with an inductive definition"
[others' labels are taken in]
   ↓   misrecognition / internalisation = AN ILLUSION (not "acceptance")
   ↓
[the person perceives "this is me" = a colonised deductive self]
   ↓
[conduct is generated from there]
   ↓
[others' observation corroborates the label]   ← looping effect / self-fulfilling
   ↓                                              prophecy
[the record fixes it]   ← the support worker's lens writes the person's
   (loop)                  inductive self
```

### Where language sits

**"Deprivation of language experience reinforces this fixing."**
**Conversely, holding the deductive self in language structurally prevents the
internalisation of labels.**

**But — language is a condition, not a goal.**

## 6. The two prohibitions

The two most important lines in this theory.

### 6-1. Do not chase the fantasy of "recovering a pure deductive self"

**There is no "unsullied true self" to be protected.** Because the deductive self
**itself** may already be colonised.

**What support should build is not the true self — it is interpretive resources
and the right to correct.**

### 6-2. Do not set "cultivating the deductive self" as the goal of support

> An intervention model of "making a subject who has no deductive self cultivate
> or recover one" **structurally inverts into a new paternalism — the imposition of
> a "correct self".** It is a re-run of the meritocratic figure of "enlightening
> the ignorant". **The moment that goal is set, a person judged to lack a self
> falls once again into being the object of a support worker's enlightenment.**

**The correct goal is this.**

> **To build, together with the person, the interpretive resources — words and
> indicators — with which they can make meaning of their own experience; and to
> guarantee their standing right to correct the system's errors.**

The framework is **supported decision-making (SDM / CRPD Article 12)**. Autonomy is
re-read not as "the capacity to decide rationally on one's own" but as **an
interdependent process with others** — because vulnerability and dependence are
**the universal human condition**.

## 7. Society's side — the three factors

**Three independent structural indicators** for reading the weakening of the
social substrate.

| Factor | What it points at |
|---|---|
| **Anomie** | Slackening of norms; detachment from social bonds. **Having nothing to lose destroys social predictability.** |
| **Hubris** | **Excess and arrogance on the side holding power.** **Power exempts itself from inspection.** |
| **Hypnos** | **Resignation, silence, cognitive numbing.** Its content is **the colonisation of self-concept** (§5). |

**The three factors are independent and parallel.** Do not re-collapse them into
two poles. Do not arrange them in temporal order (do not make "Anomie → Hubris →
Hypnos" into stages).

### The public-health reframe

**The three factors are read as collective determinants of social infrastructure,
not as individual moral failures.** Do not blame cholera on the individual; **fix
the water supply.** Same structure.

But — **the ordering discipline applies here too.** Resilience is an argument that
supports institutionalisation; it is not the grounds.

## 8. Who repays the off-balance-sheet liability

| Layer | Whose work | What it can do |
|---|---|---|
| **N = 1 (the individual case)** | **The provider, the support worker** | **Making it visible, and no further** (records in the person's name, the right to correct, exercised agency) |
| **Social security (the institution)** | **Law and institutions arrived at by agreement** | **Internalisation and repayment** (the institution takes the cost on) |

**A provider can only make it visible. Recognising a liability is not repaying
it.** Moving the equilibrium requires a payer and rights enforcement that **make
agency support rewarded.** That is the political layer. **Do not over-promise.**

## 9. The structure at the very bottom — the undersupply of decision support

A person's **decision-making (agency)** is a good whose **benefit falls on the
person and whose burden falls, divided, on each of the surrounding actors.**

Family, medicine, welfare and administration each **minimise their own burden.**
**And so each one's local optimum converges on "the person not deciding".**

**No one is rewarded for supplying decision support, so no one supplies it.**
**It is the undersupply of a public good whose beneficiary is the most powerless
actor.**

### Put in accounting terms

**What a system calls "efficiency" is the degree to which it has pushed things
off-balance-sheet onto the person.** Those around lighten their own books, and the
cost is piled onto **the unrecognised ledger of the one who cannot refuse — the
person.**

### The inversion of measurement — this is what drives the design

**"Absence of friction" must not be a performance indicator.**
**Zero complaints, everyone compliant, high retention — that can be the most
oppressive state of all.**

**Count the opposite** — **exercised agency** (**corrections** to records /
**refusals** / **recorded objections** / **leaving** / **withheld consent**) is
recognised as **a signal of health.**

**Friction is not failure. It is evidence that an oppressive equilibrium is being
broken.**

### And the blade points this way

**An SoE provider is the same kind of actor as those four.** Remuneration tied to
retention rates wants **"a person who does not decide, who stays where they are
put, who is low-burden".**

**So SoE's only difference is not "good intentions".**
**It is recognising its own off-balance-sheet liability against its own
incentive.**
**Miss that, and SoE becomes the fifth oppressor.**

## 10. Goodwill-independence

**Do not make the quality of support depend on the goodwill of the support worker.
Secure it through structure and the allocation of the burden of proof.**

### The decisive example

"Did you understand?" → "Yes."

**That "yes" is, for the person, a defence that protects them; and for the worker,
material for exoneration.**

**The same "yes": a defence to one, an exoneration to the other. There is no ill
will. It looks sincere.**
**That is precisely why goodwill cannot be depended on.** A sincere person,
remaining sincere, structurally harms the person.
**What stops that is not goodwill. It is structure.**

### What securing it through structure means

- **Put the burden of proof on the side with power** (the duty of transparency is
  proportional to power).
- **Fix the subject of a record to the support worker themselves.**
- **Cut conflicts of interest externally, not by internal self-discipline.**

### Prohibitions

- Do not use "our staff are all good people" as grounds for quality.
- **SoE itself is not an exception.**

## 11. Why do it — a craftsman's pride

**The motive is not a mission, not a calling, not social justice. It is the pride
of a craftsman in welfare support.** And, honestly, it is also **the selfish want
to let a child see the back of a father who has a craftsman's pride.**

- **Do not narrate the support worker as a hero or a person of destiny.** Heroic
  tales end up legitimating **dependence on goodwill.**
- **The work is a quasi-mandate contract.** The person is the mandator; the support
  worker owes performance of the agreed added value.

### What that pride entails

> **"A mechanism that disappears when I die" is itself against a sherpa's pride.**

**A guide's success is the climber no longer needing the guide.** Support that
keeps making itself necessary is **support for oneself.**
**So what is built is "a mechanism in which I am not needed".** That is not
ambition; it is **pride in its completed form.**

---

## 12. Conclusion — being a needle

### What SoE is for

> **A device that hands the intuition of practitioners and of the people
> themselves a field on which it can win or lose.**

**The proposer's intuition does not need to be correct.** Corroborated or refuted,
**either way is a result.**

**The guard is not made into a device that automatically produces significance.**
Laundering an intuition in the garb of rigour would be only self-affirmation
wearing the face of science. **Putting it into a refutable form** — that alone
preserves this device's reason for existing.

### Being a needle

SoE is not a hammer for remaking society. **It is a needle, for sewing.** The hole
it passes through is small, the thread it carries is thin. **But if it does not
pass, the cloth does not join.**

**Do not swing wide. Go in deep.** And — **a needle is, in the end, put down.**
